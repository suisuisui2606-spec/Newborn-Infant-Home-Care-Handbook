# Why Babies Cry

> A caregiver guide for newborn care, common observations, warning signs, and early interaction.

## Important Notes

- This document is for family communication and daily care reference only. It does not replace medical diagnosis or treatment.

- When unsure, record the time, photos/videos, feeding amount, wet diapers, stool changes, and temperature, then contact a pediatrician.

- For babies under 3 months, fever, breathing difficulty, bluish color, hard-to-wake sleepiness, persistent refusal to feed, clearly reduced urination, green vomit, or large amounts of blood in stool require urgent medical attention.

- Safe sleep always means: on the back, alone, on a flat and firm surface, with no pillows, quilts, bumper pads, stuffed toys, or positioning products.

## Unified Care Principles


- Look at the baby’s overall condition: alertness, feeding, breathing, wet diapers, skin color, and weight trend are often more important than one isolated symptom.
- Before using any medicine, including creams, eye drops, probiotics, and vitamin products, confirm the age range, dosage, and whether it overlaps with other products.
- Safe sleep should always be on the back, alone, on a flat and firm surface, with no pillows, quilts, bumper pads, stuffed toys, or sleep-positioning products.
- If unsure, record the time, photos/videos, feeding amount, diaper count, and temperature, then contact a pediatrician without delay.

## Common Newborn Questions

### Physiological Jaundice


Breastfeeding-related jaundice may last longer than jaundice in formula-fed babies, so the key is to watch the trend.

- In many full-term babies, physiological jaundice appears around day 2-3 after birth, peaks around day 4-5, and then gradually improves. Whether it is normal cannot be judged only by appearance or age in days; transcutaneous or blood bilirubin testing may be needed.
- Breastfeeding-related jaundice may come from early insufficient intake or later breast milk jaundice when the baby feeds and grows well. For early jaundice, first check latch, feeding frequency, urine output, and weight change. Do not stop breastfeeding or give sugar water on your own.
- If jaundice improves and then becomes deeper again, it should be taken seriously, but diagnosis should be made by a doctor based on age, bilirubin level, rate of rise, and direct bilirubin.

**Seek medical care promptly:** jaundice within the first 24 hours; rapidly worsening jaundice; jaundice spreading to arms, legs, palms, or soles; hard-to-wake sleepiness; refusal to feed; high-pitched crying; fever; dark urine; or pale/gray stool.

### Skin Findings

#### Mongolian Spots


Mongolian spots are common blue-gray or dark patches, often seen on the buttocks or lower back. They usually fade with age, often becoming much lighter around 2-3 years. Taking a photo record of the location and size can help avoid confusion with bruises later.

#### Salmon Patches


Salmon patches are common vascular marks. They often become lighter or temporarily disappear when pressed. Many fade within 3-4 months, though some last beyond 1 year. They are often seen on the forehead, eyelids, bridge of the nose, or back of the neck.

If the mark is very dark, sharply bordered, does not blanch with pressure, keeps enlarging, or covers a large facial area, ask a doctor to check whether it is another type of vascular birthmark.

#### Newborn Rash and Toxic Erythema


Newborn rash and erythema toxicum are common and often related to the skin adapting to the outside environment and normal immune response. “Toxic erythema” does not mean poisoning and usually resolves on its own.

- Keep the skin clean and dry. Do not squeeze, pick, or apply antibiotics without medical advice.
- Seek medical care if blisters appear in clusters, become obvious, break and drain pus, or if the baby has fever, poor energy, or reduced feeding.

#### Sebaceous Gland Hyperplasia, Milia, and Heat Rash


- Sebaceous gland hyperplasia is often seen on the nose and is related to hormone effects. It may be more noticeable in boys. Clean gently with warm water only. Do not squeeze, pick, scrub, or use acne products.
- Milia and small newborn bumps usually go away on their own within weeks to months. Do not squeeze them.
- Heat rash is related to blocked sweat ducts, overheating, and sweating. It often appears on the head, neck, upper chest, back, and skin folds. Use lighter clothing, ventilation, gentle bathing, and pat the skin dry. Avoid heavy ointments and talcum powder.

**Seek care:** pus bumps, obvious swelling or pain, oozing, fever, or no improvement after cooling and basic care.

#### Intertrigo


Intertrigo often appears in skin folds such as the neck, armpits, and groin. It is related to moisture, friction, and poor ventilation, and is more common in chubbier babies.

- Clean gently and pat completely dry every day.
- Allow short periods of air exposure.
- Do not scrub.
- For mild redness, a thin layer of fragrance-free barrier ointment may help.
- If the folds are bright red with small scattered red spots around them, yeast infection may be present and a doctor should advise treatment.

#### Newborn Acne and Eczema


Newborn acne often appears as small red bumps and whiteheads, and may be more common in boys. Medication is usually not needed. Mupirocin and fusidic acid are antibiotics and are not routine treatments for newborn acne. They should only be used if a doctor suspects bacterial infection.

Eczema often appears as dry, rough, red, itchy skin. Severe eczema may ooze or crust. Basic care includes gentle cleansing and frequent moisturizing: bathe with warm water, keep bath time short, and apply fragrance-free cream or ointment within 3 minutes after bathing. Repeat moisturizing as needed.

**Seek care:** significant itchiness, sleep disturbance, yellow crusting or oozing, spreading rash, or repeated flare-ups. Do not use mixed medicated creams long-term without medical advice.

#### Impetigo


Impetigo can be caused by bacterial infection and may spread. It may appear as pus-filled bumps, broken skin, honey-colored crusting, or surrounding redness. Because newborn immune systems are immature, it should not simply be watched at home.

**Care principle:** do not share towels or clothing with other children, wash hands before and after contact, do not squeeze the rash, and seek medical care promptly. Fever, poor energy, reduced feeding, or rapid spread requires urgent evaluation.

### Medication and Skin Care


- Calamine lotion may temporarily relieve some itching, but it can dry the skin. It is not suitable for oozing or broken skin and should not be used near the eyes, mouth, nose, or over large areas without medical advice.
- Fule cream is a specific institutional preparation in some hospitals and should be used only according to the prescribing institution’s instructions. It should not be treated as a universal rash medicine.
- The main skin-care approach is: gentle cleansing, timely moisturizing, reducing irritation, and observing changes. The same rash name may have different causes. If unsure, take photos and consult a pediatrician or dermatologist.

### Eye Findings


- Epicanthal folds, brief crossed-eye appearance, eye-rolling, or pseudo-strabismus can be seen in young babies. Watch whether it is persistent and whether both eyes move symmetrically.
- Temporary retinal bleeding can occur after vaginal delivery and often resolves, but it should be followed according to medical advice if detected during newborn screening or examination. Visible bleeding on the white of the eye is often subconjunctival hemorrhage and also usually resolves.

**Seek care:** clearly asymmetric eye movement, persistent strabismus, no response to light, very red eye, cloudy cornea, swollen eyelid, or obvious increase in discharge.

### Mouth Findings


- Epstein pearls: common, usually disappear within weeks, and should not be picked or punctured.
- Sucking pads: raised fatty pads on both inner cheeks that help with sucking and are normal.
- Natal or extra teeth: if loose, interfering with feeding, or rubbing the tongue, they should be checked by a doctor.
- Tongue-tie: varies widely. Most babies do not need treatment. The key is whether it affects latch, feeding efficiency, maternal nipple pain, or weight gain.

### Heart Murmur


Some newborns may have a soft systolic murmur during the first few days after birth, possibly related to the transition from fetal circulation to newborn circulation. It should not be simplified as “wait until after 3 months.”

**Seek care promptly:** feeding fatigue, excessive sweating during feeds, rapid breathing, bluish lips, poor weight gain, poor energy, or if a doctor recommends follow-up.

### Body Shape Variations

#### Foot Position


Some foot turning is a flexible positional variation from the womb and can be gently moved back toward neutral. True clubfoot is often stiff and hard to correct and should be evaluated early by pediatric orthopedics. Do not forcefully stretch the foot at home.

#### Skull Sutures and Fontanelles


Gently touching from the forehead toward the top of the head may reveal the soft anterior fontanelle and skull sutures. The posterior fontanelle is smaller and may close soon after birth. A normal fontanelle may move slightly with breathing or crying.

- A clearly sunken fontanelle when calm and upright, especially with fewer wet diapers, dry mouth, or poor energy, may suggest dehydration.
- A persistently bulging fontanelle when calm, especially with fever, vomiting, sleepiness, or high-pitched crying, needs urgent evaluation.
- Local skull depression, obvious head asymmetry, or hard ridging along sutures should be checked by pediatrics or neurosurgery.

#### Temple Depression, Kneecap, Ear Shape, and Breast Swelling


- Mild temple depression is often related to skull shape and fat distribution, not simply sleeping position. If both sides are similar, head circumference follows the growth curve, and feeding/energy are normal, it can be followed at well-baby visits. Sudden, clearly asymmetric, or trauma-related changes need medical care.
- The kneecap is mainly cartilage at first and gradually ossifies later. It does not “grow out” because of crawling. Home checking or training is usually unnecessary.
- Ear shape differences that do not affect hearing, glasses, or earphones are often cosmetic. If ear molding is considered, the early newborn period is usually the better window; consult a specialist.
- Due to maternal hormones, both boys and girls may have mild breast swelling or a small amount of milky discharge for several weeks. Do not squeeze or massage.

### Colic


Typical infant colic is often described by the “rule of 3”: crying for about 3 hours a day, at least 3 days a week, for more than 3 weeks. More importantly, observe the whole baby: sudden intense crying often occurs in the evening or at night, with clenched fists, kicking, arching, or a red face, while feeding, growth, and alertness are generally normal between episodes.

- It often begins a few weeks after birth and gradually improves around 3-4 months.
- First rule out hunger, diaper discomfort, overheating/cold, tiredness, need to burp, tight clothing, and illness.
- Feed according to hunger cues. Avoid adding feeds every time the baby cries, as overfeeding can worsen discomfort.
- Try holding, gentle rocking, white noise, swaddling, gentle clockwise belly massage, gas-relief movements, and supervised “football hold” while awake. Try only one or two methods at a time and watch the baby’s response.

**Safe sleep:** tummy position is only for awake tummy time with full adult supervision. All sleep should be on the back, alone, on a flat and firm sleep surface. Do not let the baby sleep tummy-down on an adult’s chest or belly, and do not sleep with the baby on a sofa or armchair. Never shake a baby.

**Seek urgent care:** fever, repeated projectile or green vomiting, blood in stool, hard swollen belly, breathing difficulty, hard-to-wake sleepiness, refusal to feed with fewer wet diapers, a cry that suddenly sounds very different, or a caregiver’s strong feeling that something is wrong.

### Vitamin D and Other Common Questions

#### Vitamin D


Vitamin D supports calcium/phosphorus metabolism and bone health. Many guidelines recommend 400 IU vitamin D daily for breastfed or partially breastfed infants. Families in China should follow the birth hospital, well-baby clinic, and product instructions.

- Formula is usually fortified with vitamin D. Whether extra supplementation is needed depends on the baby’s actual daily formula intake and the product label.
- Vitamin AD products and vitamin D-only products are different plans. Do not stack multiple products containing vitamin A or D.
- Measure drops accurately. Do not double the dose after a missed dose. Premature, low-birth-weight, or liver/biliary disease babies need individualized medical advice.

#### Spitting Bubbles and Temperature


Spitting bubbles is often related to increased saliva, oral activity, or mild reflux. By itself, it does not mean pneumonia. Newborn pneumonia may include foamy saliva, but usually also has breathing or general condition changes.

**Seek care promptly:** bubbles plus refusal to feed, weak cry, gray face, bluish lips, pale-gray skin, cold limbs, irritability, shallow/fast/irregular breathing, or flaring nostrils.

Measure temperature when the baby is calm, not right after bathing, and not overly bundled. Record the site, method, time, and value. For babies under 3 months, a temperature of 38.0°C or higher requires immediate medical contact or emergency evaluation. Do not give fever medicine first and continue observing without advice.

#### Hand Sucking and Pacifiers


For young babies, hand sucking is often self-soothing and sensory exploration. It usually does not need to be stopped. Keep hands clean, nails trimmed, and avoid loose threads from mittens. If the habit continues long after teeth erupt, affects bite alignment, or causes broken/infected skin, consult a dentist.

Pacifiers may be used in moderation. For breastfed babies, introduce after latch, intake, and weight gain are stable. It can be offered at sleep time; if it falls out after the baby falls asleep, it does not need to be put back repeatedly. Do not dip it in sugar or honey, do not tie it around the neck, and do not replace it with a bottle nipple.

#### Bow Legs and First Haircut


Mild bow legs are common before age 2, and leg alignment changes with growth. Do not bind the legs or force them straight. Seek medical care if the legs are clearly asymmetric, worsening, only one-sided, different in length or thickness, limited in hip abduction, associated with abnormal walking, or painful.

Shaving newborn hair does not make hair thicker or denser. If trimming is needed because of sweating or scalp care, use safe tools and avoid shaving too close to the skin.

### Head Shape


From 0-4 months, head shape may be affected by preferred sleeping direction, torticollis, and limited awake movement. The key to preventing or improving mild flat head is always placing the baby on the back for sleep, while increasing supervised tummy time, changing holding and feeding positions, and encouraging the baby to turn both ways with voices and faces.

- Use more tummy time while awake; sleep remains on the back.
- Use sound and visual cues to encourage turning in both directions.
- Positioning pillows, head-shaping pillows, or sleep positioners are not recommended unless specifically advised by a medical professional.
- If head shape is clearly abnormal, accompanied by torticollis or limited movement, or if caregivers are worried, seek evaluation at well-baby care, rehabilitation, or a specialist clinic.

### Nasal Congestion and Boogers


Babies have short, narrow nasal passages and rich blood supply in the nasal lining, so a small amount of mucus can cause noisy breathing. Cold/hot air, dust, viruses, or bacteria can irritate the nose.

- Clean only visible mucus at the nostril opening. Do not insert cotton swabs deep into the nose.
- For mild congestion, use infant-appropriate drug-free saline drops. Let the mucus soften and allow sneezing to help. If truly needed, use a nasal aspirator gently and briefly.
- Babies under 6 months usually should not be given extra water; focus on breast milk or formula intake.
- Humidifiers should be cleaned and refilled daily to prevent mold and bacteria.

**Avoid:** pinching both nostrils to blow the nose, using tweezers to remove boogers, or using adult nasal sprays/decongestants without medical advice.

**Seek care:** labored breathing, chest retractions, bluish lips, clearly reduced feeding, fewer wet diapers, fever under 3 months, or worsening congestion.

### Green Stool and Abnormal Stool


Stool color is related to food, bile, and gut bacteria. Breastfed babies may have more acidic stool, and bacterial changes can make it light green. If feeding and growth are good, green stool alone is usually not concerning.

**Abnormal stool reminders:**

- Small streaks of bright red blood may come from anal fissure or cow’s milk protein-related colitis and should be discussed with a pediatrician. Large, repeated bleeding or bleeding with poor energy, bloating, vomiting, or fever requires urgent care.
- White, gray, or clay-colored stool needs prompt medical care, especially with jaundice or dark urine, to check for cholestasis.
- Black tarry stool, outside the first days of meconium or iron supplementation, needs urgent evaluation.
- Foamy stool does not always mean lactose intolerance. If weight gain, feeding, and energy are good, observation may be reasonable; frequent diarrhea, fewer wet diapers, poor energy, or poor weight gain should be discussed with a doctor.
- Pasty, hard stool or anal fissure should be judged together with feeding type, stool frequency, and the baby’s overall condition.

### Diaper Rash


The most common type is irritant diaper dermatitis, usually affecting raised areas that contact urine and stool, while folds are often less involved. If the rash matches the diaper contact area with clear borders and itching, contact allergy is possible. If folds are bright red with satellite red spots, yeast infection may be present.

Care points:

- Change diapers frequently and clean promptly after urine or stool.
- Pat dry gently after cleaning; do not rub repeatedly.
- Allow short periods of air exposure.
- Diaper cream mainly works as a barrier to reduce friction and separate skin from urine and stool. Check that the layer is thick enough after each diaper change.
- If stool has not stuck to the cream, add another layer. If stool is present, wipe off the stool and reapply. If very messy, clean and reapply.
- Choose ointment or cream barrier products. Thin lotions usually provide weaker protection.

**Seek care:** no improvement after 2-3 days of correct care, broken/oozing skin, pus bumps, rapid spread, significant pain, or fever. Do not mix antifungals, antibiotics, or steroid creams without medical advice.

### Watery Eyes


Tears while crying are normal. Persistent tearing in one or both eyes when not crying, especially with more discharge, may be related to congenital nasolacrimal duct obstruction. After a doctor demonstrates the method, gentle massage downward from the inner corner near the nose may be used, along with keeping the eye area clean. Many cases improve with growth.

**Seek care:** very red eye, swollen eyelid, light sensitivity, cloudy cornea, or painful swelling near the inner eye corner with fever.

### Redness or Pus on Left Upper Arm


Redness and a small pus bump on the left upper arm may be a local reaction after BCG vaccination. Do not squeeze, pick, or disinfect aggressively at home. Keep the area clean and dry.

**Seek care:** clearly enlarging redness or ulcer, persistent pus drainage, obvious swelling in the armpit on the same side, fever, poor energy, or a white pus head larger than about 1.5 cm.

## Early Education

### Newborn Brain Development


Gentle sensory input, warm and predictable care, and timely responses help babies build a sense of security and provide experience for sensory, motor, and language development. The goal is not “training,” but brief interaction when the baby is awake and comfortable. Stop when the baby turns away, yawns, frowns, or becomes fussy.

### Vision


Newborns see faces best at about 20-30 cm. Looking at caregivers’ faces and hearing familiar voices are excellent interactions. High-contrast cards can be one kind of visual stimulation, but they are not required. Show one card at a time, move slowly, and stop when the baby turns away.

If around 2 months the baby rarely looks at faces, does not respond to light, or tracks very asymmetrically, consult pediatrics or ophthalmology.

In very young babies, grasping is often reflexive. Do not keep toys stuffed in the baby’s hand for long periods.

### Hearing and Language


Music can be a gentle daily stimulus and a way to build rhythm and bonding, but it should not be treated as the only way to “increase intelligence.” For newborns, caregiver voices, responses, and repeated interaction matter more.

Language development varies between babies:

- Around 2 months: vowel-like sounds such as “ah,” “oh,” or “eh.”
- 3-4 months: more cooing and response to adult voices.
- 5-6 months: some consonant sounds such as g/k/b/p may appear.
- 7-8 months: sounds like mama/baba may appear but usually without clear meaning.
- 9-10 months: mama/baba may start to become meaningful.
- 11-12 months: more connected syllables and baby-like “speech.”

When talking to the baby: use clear mouth movements, slow speed, short sentences, expressive faces, repetition, and pauses for the baby to respond.

### Touch


Touch is one of the earliest developing senses. Gentle touch can soothe the baby, support bonding, and help the baby explore the world through the skin.

Before massage, keep hands warm and nails smooth. Watch whether the baby agrees: relaxation, looking toward you, or leaning in means you may continue; stiffness, turning away, or crying means stop.

The massage order does not need to be strict. Start with areas the baby accepts more easily, such as legs and feet, then arms, chest/belly, back, and face. Belly massage should be gentle and avoided when the umbilical area is not healed, skin is broken, or the baby seems uncomfortable.

For grasping practice, gently touch the back of the hand and let it open naturally. Allow short contact with safe textures. Do not force objects into the hand or keep them there for long.

### Gross and Fine Motor Development


Infant motor development generally progresses from head control to trunk control and then standing/walking, and from reflexive grasping to intentional reaching and fine movements. Every baby develops at a different pace.

- Avoid overprotection and long periods of restricted movement.
- Use supervised tummy time while awake to support neck, shoulder, and back strength.
- Do not treat crying as exercise. When the baby cries, first respond and check hunger, tiredness, diaper, temperature, gas, and discomfort.
- Hand-eye coordination develops gradually through looking at faces, looking at toys, and briefly touching safe objects.
- Later self-feeding depends on gradual development of hand skills, oral skills, and supported sitting. It does not need to be trained during the newborn period.

## Quick Medical Warning Signs

| Situation | What to Do |
|---|---|
| Baby under 3 months with temperature >= 38.0°C | Contact doctor or emergency care immediately |
| Breathing difficulty, chest retractions, bluish lips | Emergency care |
| Hard to wake, poor energy, persistent refusal to feed | Urgent medical evaluation |
| Clearly reduced wet diapers | Contact pediatrician promptly |
| Green vomit, repeated projectile vomiting | Emergency care |
| Large blood in stool, black tarry stool, gray-white stool | Urgent evaluation |
| Rapidly spreading rash, pus, fever with rash | Medical evaluation |
| Caregiver feels something is clearly wrong | Trust the observation and seek help |
